01
About the project

Food, land
and sovereignty

This project examines food sovereignty in Ecuador through an environmental, historical, social, and cultural lens.

It explores how colonial land systems, resource extraction, environmental change, Indigenous food systems, and grassroots organizing continue to shape Ecuador's relationship with food and land.

02
Research

Questions we explored

  • How have colonial land systems shaped Ecuadorian food systems?
  • How do environmental changes affect food sovereignty?
  • How do Indigenous agricultural practices contribute to ecological resilience?
  • How can food sovereignty be incorporated into the Pitzer in Ecuador program?
03
My contribution

Geography as a way of connecting evidence

I contributed to the research, writing, geographic analysis, and visual communication developed for this interdisciplinary project. My role focused on connecting environmental and social questions to Ecuadorian geography and helping translate research into an accessible spatial narrative.

Project outcome

From research
to proposal

Translating research into an educational proposal.

Our research resulted in a proposal for incorporating food sovereignty into the Pitzer in Ecuador Semester Program.

The proposal includes field-based learning opportunities and potential collaboration with local organizations working around food systems, Indigenous rights, agroecology, and environmental justice.
