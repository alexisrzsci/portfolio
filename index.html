Research

Academic and collaborative research connecting environmental questions, ecological field methods, experimental approaches, data management, quantitative analysis, and interdisciplinary approaches to understanding ecological and human–environment systems.

Collaborative Arthropod Research

My research experience includes hands-on ecological field and laboratory work, specimen processing, taxonomic identification, data management, and scientific communication.

I contributed to a long-term study examining changes in beetle assemblages over a ten-year period at the Robert J. Bernard Field Station in Southern California. My work included specimen processing, inventory, family-level identification based on morphological characteristics, ecological data organization, and additional project support.

Species-level identification and the final community analysis were completed by other members of the research team, allowing my contribution to be presented accurately within the broader collaborative research process.

About me

I am an undergraduate researcher interested in geospatial science, GIS, environmental research, and the role of spatial data in understanding complex environmental systems.

My academic interests sit at the intersection of geography, environmental science, spatial analysis, and visual communication. I am particularly interested in transforming environmental data into clear maps, quantitative analyses, and research narratives that make complex questions easier to understand.
